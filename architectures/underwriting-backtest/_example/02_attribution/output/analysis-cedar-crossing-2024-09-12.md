# Underwriting Attribution: Cedar Crossing Apartments
Record reference: 01_reconcile/output/record-cedar-crossing-2024-09-12.md   Outcome: Outperformed

## Canonical Questions
1. **Material assumptions:** Exit cap rate (decisive), lease-up/stabilization pace, and — secondary
   — rent growth and capex.
2. **Underwritten vs. actual:** Exit cap 5.75% → 5.10% (−65 bps). Lease-up 12 → 18 months (+6).
   Rent growth 3.5% → 3.2% (−0.3 pts/yr). Capex $18.0k → $19.1k/unit (+$1.1k).
3. **Headline gap:** 16.0% → 22.3% IRR (+6.3 pts); 1.9x → 2.3x.
4. **Cause class per miss:** Exit cap — **exogenous** (sector-wide multifamily cap compression
   2019→2024). Lease-up — **forecasting error** (our 12-month assumption was optimistic), with a
   partial **execution/exogenous** overlay from the 2020 leasing disruption. Rent growth —
   forecasting (modest). Capex — execution (modest overrun).
5. **Skill vs. luck:** The IRR beat is **luck-dominant.** Decomposing the +6.3 pts: roughly +8 pts
   from the exit-cap compression we neither underwrote nor created, against roughly −2 pts of drag
   from our own slower lease-up and lighter rent growth. Stripped of the cap move, this deal would
   have realized *below* its 16% underwriting. We believe this read: the exit-cap compression was
   market-wide across Phoenix multifamily in the period, not an asset-specific premium we engineered.
   [confidence: high]
6. **Systematic or one-off:** Exit-cap compression — exogenous, not a Ridgeline skill, and not
   something to bank as repeatable. The lease-up miss — **systematic**: it matches the direction of
   our other multifamily value-add deals (see store). Rent/capex misses — one-off / immaterial.
7. **Full information?** Mostly. We underwrote a reasonable 2019 exit cap; we did not (and could
   not) forecast the compression. The lease-up assumption, though, was optimistic on information we
   had — comparable renovations in the submarket were already running longer than 12 months.
8. **Decisive driver / repeatable?** Exit cap. **Not repeatable** — it is a rate-cycle artifact. We
   should not assume it recurs, and we should not credit it to the thesis.
9. **(N/A — outperformed.)**
10. **Calibration adjustment:** (a) Underwrite Phoenix multifamily value-add exit caps **flat to
    +25 bps over going-in**, not compressed — do not bank cap-rate tailwind. (b) Extend the
    multifamily value-add **lease-up/stabilization assumption by ~6 months** (12 → ~18) for renovation
    plans of this scope.

## Skill vs. Luck
The headline 22.3% reads like a strong value-add execution. It was not. The exit-cap compression
(−65 bps) supplied the entire beat and then some; Ridgeline's own operating execution —
stabilization six months slow, rents slightly behind — was a net drag. Honest read:
**luck-dominant.** Banking this as proof of underwriting skill would be the exact error the loop
exists to prevent. [high]

## Why (proposed)
Cedar Crossing outperformed because it sold into a materially compressed cap environment, not
because the business plan executed to underwriting. [exogenous; high] The value-add execution
itself underperformed plan on the dimension we control most — lease-up pace — and that miss is
consistent with our other multifamily deals, so it is a forecasting bias, not bad luck. [forecasting,
systematic; medium-high] Causal driver of the *beat*: exit cap (exogenous). Causal driver of the
*self-inflicted drag*: lease-up assumption (systematic forecasting error). The 2020 disruption
explains *part* of the lease-up delay but not the pattern, since the same direction shows up in
non-2020 deals.

## Against the Store
**Strengthens the exit-cap pattern** (realized returns leaning on compression we did not
underwrite) — this is a third multifamily/industrial record on that thread and should **promote it
to a stated pattern.** **Strengthens the multifamily lease-up pattern** (our 12-month assumption
runs ~6 months optimistic) — a second multifamily record (with Maple Grove). Does not contradict
any pattern. Sharpens the skill-vs-luck ledger toward "much of the recent track record is beta."

## Implied Calibration Adjustment
- **Exit cap (Phoenix MF value-add):** underwrite flat-to-+25 bps vs. going-in; stop assuming
  compression. Flag to deal-screening economics-assumptions and deal-pipeline underwriting.
- **Lease-up (MF value-add, full reno):** add ~6 months to the standard stabilization assumption.
