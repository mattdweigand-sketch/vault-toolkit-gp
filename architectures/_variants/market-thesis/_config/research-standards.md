## Source Vetting
[How to rank a source's reliability. Examples:
- First-party data providers (with their known lag) over sell-side forecasts.
- Sell-side/broker research weighted for its position (a seller's lean).
- Named, dated sources over headlines and anecdote.
- How to handle conflicting sources: surface both, weight by reliability, do
  not silently pick one.]

## What Counts as Supported
[The bar a claim must clear to be stated as evidence rather than inference. A
figure tagged to a named source at a stated date is evidence. An interpretation
of several figures is inference and must be labeled as such.]

## Confidence Levels
[The firm's vocabulary for marking confidence on inferences (e.g., high /
medium / low, or a stated convention). Used consistently so a reader can compare
conviction across claims and across theses.]

## Never-Do List
[Testable rules every thesis must pass. Examples:
- Never state a market figure not tagged to a named source (Constraint 09).
- Never present an inference or forecast as an observed fact.
- Never publish without a risk case and a falsification condition.
- Never let an LP-facing variant assert more than the internal thesis supports.]
