# Hold/Sell Case: Maple Commons

**To:** Northpoint Fund III Investment Committee
**From:** Deal team (J. Okafor)
**Date:** 2026-06-11 (for the 2026-06-18 IC)
**Decision requested:** Market Maple Commons for sale in Q3 2026, or hold to the 2028 debt
maturity.

---

## Recommendation

**Sell in Q3 2026, subject to confirming the exit-cap assumption below.** The value-add plan has
substantially played out — occupancy is 93% and renovated units are hitting their rent premium
[S05] — so most of the lift this asset was acquired to capture has been captured. Selling now
harvests that gain near the top of the business plan rather than carrying execution and rate risk
to a 2028 exit for a modeled return that, on the current assumptions, is not materially better.

This recommendation rests on one assumption the IC should confirm before acting: the exit cap. The
current model uses 5.50% [S03]; an earlier version used 5.25% [superseded, S02]; the change is
undocumented. At 5.50% the sell case is clearly the stronger one. If the committee's view is that
the asset trades closer to 5.25%, the gap between hold and sell narrows and the decision is closer.
See Open Items.

---

## The sell case

- **Current position.** Cost basis $46.3M; outstanding debt $31.0M (fixed, maturing 2028-09); NAV
  $55.4M as of 2026-03-31 [S01].
- **Modeled sale.** At a 5.50% exit cap, the current model puts gross value at ~$57M, with net
  proceeds to the fund after the debt payoff and transaction costs [S03]. A broker's opinion of
  value, treated as an outside data point rather than a valuation, puts the range at $58–62M at an
  implied ~5.40% cap [S04].
- **Why now.** The renovation-and-relet plan is largely complete and rents are at the underwritten
  premium [S05]. The thesis has delivered. Holding a stabilized asset to 2028 converts a value-add
  bet that worked into a core hold — a different risk/return profile than the fund underwrote.

## The hold case

- **The argument.** Maple Commons is a stabilized, well-occupied asset with durable income [S05].
  Holding to the 2028 debt maturity defers transaction costs and keeps capital invested in a known
  performer.
- **What it would require.** Continued (modest) rent growth, a $1.1M capital reserve over the hold
  — including two HVAC replacements flagged in the next 18 months [S05] — and an exit at the same
  cap in 2028 [S03]. The model's hold-period return, on these assumptions, is only modestly above
  the sell case, and it carries two more years of interest-rate and execution risk.
- **A possible upside not in the numbers.** The internal email thread refers to a refinance quote
  [S06] that, if real, could improve hold-period returns. No refinance term sheet is in the source
  set, so the hold case here excludes it. If the IC wants the hold case fully developed, that quote
  should be retrieved. [⚠️ NOT SUPPORTED — refinance quote referenced but not in the source set;
  see Open Items.]

## Risks and what would change the recommendation

- **Exit cap (the swing factor).** A 25 bps move in the cap is worth roughly $3M of value on this
  asset and is most of the gap between the two cases. The recommendation to sell holds at 5.50%; at
  5.25% the decision is closer. This is the assumption to confirm first.
- **In-place income rests on an unverified source.** The model's forward math is built on a
  February 2026 rent roll that is referenced but not in the source set [⚠️ NOT SUPPORTED — see
  Open Items]. Occupancy in the AM report is consistent with a healthy in-place picture [S05], but
  it does not tie to the model line by line.
- **A 2024 appraisal in the file is stale.** It valued the asset at $54M two years ago; it
  pre-dates the current business plan and is not used here [S07]. Noted so the committee is not
  surprised by a lower number if it surfaces.
