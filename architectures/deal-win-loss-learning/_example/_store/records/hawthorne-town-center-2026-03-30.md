## Canonical Answers (summary)
Limited process run by Pinnacle for an institutional seller in wind-down needing certainty before
a fund deadline. Our thesis was a grocery-anchored repositioning. We bid $48.0M but carried a
financing contingency. The winner was ~2.5% higher *and* waived the financing out with a 30-day
close. Pinnacle's stated reason was "the higher number." Assessed: for a wind-down seller on a
clock, the waived contingency and faster close were decisive; the price gap was real but secondary.

## Validated Why
Lost on certainty, not primarily price (HIGH). A wind-down seller prioritizes a clean, on-time
close; our financing contingency signaled execution risk against a buyer who removed it. The 2.5%
price gap was closable — a stronger certainty package likely wins this even at our number.

## Stated vs. Assessed
Stated (Pinnacle): we were outbid on price. Assessed: certainty of close was the real driver — the
winner's waived contingency mattered more to a wind-down seller than the marginal price. Pinnacle's
price framing is treated skeptically here.

## Transferable Lessons
- For wind-down / deadline-driven sellers, lead with a certainty package (limit or pre-clear
  financing, compress timeline) rather than chasing price.
- Pinnacle's debrief skews to "price" — corroborate against bid structure before believing it.
