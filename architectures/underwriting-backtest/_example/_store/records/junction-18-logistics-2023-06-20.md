## Material Assumptions: Underwritten vs. Actual

- Rent growth: 4.0%/yr → 7.1%/yr (+3.1 pts, well ahead — industrial demand surge)
- Lease-up / absorption: 9 mo → 8 mo (in line; industrial leases fast)
- Going-in cap: 6.25% → 6.25% (in line)
- Capex: $7.5M → $7.4M (in line)

## Validated Why
Outperformed on two exogenous tailwinds: exit-cap compression (−70 bps) and an industrial
rent-growth surge far beyond plan. [exogenous; HIGH] A genuine sliver of skill: Ridgeline leaned
into Inland Empire industrial early on a logistics-demand thesis, so part of the rent beat reflects
a market-selection call that proved right. [skill; medium] But the cap move is pure beta, and most
of the rent surge was sector-wide, not asset-specific. Net read: mixed, leaning luck.

## Skill vs. Luck
Mixed [medium-high]. The industrial-sector thesis was a real, repeatable judgment call; the cap
compression and the magnitude of the rent surge were not. Do not generalize the full 24% as edge.

## Calibration Adjustment
- Exit cap (Inland Empire industrial): underwrite flat vs. going-in; do not bank the −70 bps.
- Rent growth (industrial): our 4% was conservative for the period, but 7% is not a forward
  assumption either — revert to a through-cycle figure, do not extrapolate the surge.
- The logistics-demand market-selection thesis is validated; weight it in sourcing.
