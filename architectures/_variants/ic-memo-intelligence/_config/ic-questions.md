# Canonical IC Questions

## The Questions
[A fixed list the analysis answers for every IC decision. Write 8-15 specific
questions. The set below is the recommended starting bank — adapt to your firm:

1. What was the recommendation, what did the committee decide (approved /
   approved-with-conditions / declined / tabled), and did the decision diverge
   from the recommendation?
2. What conditions did the committee impose, to whom, and by when?
3. What concerns or risks did the committee raise, in its own words where on the
   record?
4. What was the decisive factor — the one thing that, if different, most likely
   flips the decision? Causal or incidental?
5. Stated vs. inferred rationale: what reasons do the minutes attribute to the
   committee, and what is your assessment of what actually drove the decision?
   (The defense — keep the two distinct and say which is which.)
6. Was there dissent, and on what grounds — a formal no-vote or a stated
   reservation?
7. Are the conditions/concerns the committee's standing guardrails (recurring in
   this segment per the store) or bespoke to this deal?
8. Is this decision consistent with precedent, or a departure — did the committee
   decide a similar deal differently before?
9. What does this reveal about the committee's risk appetite in this segment (a
   leverage ceiling, a market it keeps passing on, a structure it requires)?
10. What does this imply a future memo in this segment should pre-empt, address,
    or cite?]

## Answering Rules
[How to answer for comparability:
- Answer every question, in order. Use "not on the record" where the minutes do
  not support an answer — never fabricate to fill the slot.
- Keep answers tight and factual; the implied signal for future memos has its own
  section in the analysis output.
- Distinguish the committee's stated reasoning from your inferred reading
  explicitly, and never collapse them into one.
- Mark standing vs. bespoke on every condition and concern, so a one-deal quirk
  does not get read as the committee's policy.]
