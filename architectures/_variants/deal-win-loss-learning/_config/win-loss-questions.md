# Canonical Win/Loss Questions

## The Questions
[A fixed list the analysis answers for every process, won or lost. Write 8-15
specific questions. The set below is the recommended starting bank — adapt to
your firm:

1. How did this opportunity reach us, and through which broker/intermediary or
   off-market path?
2. What was our thesis, and the price/structure we bid?
3. What was the winning bid / clearing price, and our gap to it — in price *and*
   in terms?
4. On what dimension was it won or lost: price, certainty/speed of close,
   structure, financing, reputation/relationship, or timing?
5. Broker's *stated* reason vs. our *assessed* real reason? (The spin-defense —
   keep the two distinct and say which you believe.)
6. Did we bid with full information, or blind on a key term?
7. Where in the process did we gain or lose ground (first round, best-and-final,
   last look)?
8. Did our diligence / closing reputation help or hurt?
9. If lost: what realistically would have changed it — and at what price would it
   have stopped being a deal we wanted?
10. If won: what was the decisive edge, and what nearly lost it?
11. What would we do differently in a similar process (a relationship to build, a
    term to pre-clear, a faster path to certainty)?]

## Answering Rules
[How to answer for comparability:
- Answer every question, in order. Use "unknown" where the record does not
  support an answer — never fabricate to fill the slot.
- Keep answers tight and factual; the why and the lessons have their own
  sections in the analysis output.
- Distinguish the broker's stated reason from your assessed reason explicitly,
  and never collapse them into one.]
