# Canonical Underwriting Questions

## The Questions
[A fixed list the attribution answers for every realized deal. Write 8-15
specific questions. The set below is the recommended starting bank — adapt to
your firm:

1. Which assumptions were material to the realized return — the few that actually
   moved the outcome?
2. For each material assumption: underwritten value vs. actual, and the variance
   (magnitude and direction)?
3. On the headline return (IRR / MOIC): underwritten vs. realized, and the gap?
4. For each material miss, was it a forecasting error (we got the assumption
   wrong), an execution difference (we delivered above/below plan), or exogenous
   (the market moved)?
5. Skill vs. luck: of the return variance, how much is attributable to the firm
   (thesis, execution, forecast) vs. market movement we did not create
   (cap-rate compression, rates, a sector run)? (The calibration defense — keep
   the two distinct and say which you believe.)
6. Is each material miss systematic (consistent with a bias the store already
   shows) or one-off (specific to this deal)?
7. Did we underwrite with full information, or blind on a key input?
8. If outperformed: what was the decisive driver, and was it repeatable?
9. If underperformed: what would we have needed to see at underwriting to get it
   right — and would we have still done the deal?
10. What calibration adjustment does this imply for future underwriting in this
    segment (the assumption to widen/tighten, and roughly by how much)?]

## Answering Rules
[How to answer for comparability:
- Answer every question, in order. Use "unknown" where the record does not
  support an answer — never fabricate to fill the slot.
- Keep answers tight and factual; the calibration adjustment has its own section
  in the attribution output.
- Distinguish the firm's skill from market luck explicitly, and never collapse
  them into one.
- Mark systematic vs. one-off on every material miss, so a one-deal anomaly does
  not get hard-coded into the firm's standard assumptions.]
