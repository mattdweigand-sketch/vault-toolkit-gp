## Material Assumptions: Underwritten vs. Actual
- Exit cap rate: 5.75% → 5.10% (−65 bps, compressed)
- Lease-up / stabilization: 12 mo → 18 mo (+6 mo, slower)
- Rent growth: 3.5%/yr → 3.2%/yr (−0.3 pts)
- Going-in cap: 5.25% → 5.25% (in line)
- Capex: $18.0k → $19.1k/unit (+$1.1k)

## Validated Why
Outperformed almost entirely on exit-cap compression (−65 bps) that we neither underwrote nor
created; stripped of the cap move the deal would have realized below its 16% underwriting.
[exogenous; HIGH] Our own execution ran behind plan — stabilization six months slow, rents
slightly light — a drag, not a driver. The lease-up miss is a standing forecasting bias, not the
2020 disruption alone. [forecasting, systematic; medium-high]

## Skill vs. Luck
Luck-dominant [HIGH]. The headline 22.3% reads like value-add execution; it was a compressed cap
environment. Banking it as underwriting skill would teach the firm the wrong lesson.

## Calibration Adjustment
- Exit cap (Phoenix MF value-add): underwrite flat-to-+25 bps over going-in; stop assuming
  compression.
- Lease-up (MF value-add, full reno): add ~6 months to the standard stabilization assumption.
