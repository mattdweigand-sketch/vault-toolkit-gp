# Variance Record: Cedar Crossing Apartments
Outcome: Outperformed   Realized: 2024-09-12
Model of record: IC model v3 (final), approved 2019-06-04
Asset type: Multifamily   Deal-size band: $25M–$75M   Strategy: Value-add
Market/submarket: Phoenix (Tempe)   Hold-period band: 4–7 yrs   Vintage: 2019

## Headline Return
Underwritten: 16.0% net IRR, 1.9x equity multiple (5-yr hold).
Realized: 22.3% net IRR, 2.3x equity multiple (5.3-yr hold).
Gap: +6.3 pts IRR, +0.4x multiple. Source: fund accounting final distribution waterfall,
realized at sale 2024-09-12.

## Material Assumptions: Underwritten vs. Actual
| Assumption | Underwritten | Actual | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exit cap rate | 5.75% | 5.10% | −65 bps (compressed → higher exit value) |
| Lease-up / stabilization | 12 months | 18 months | +6 months (slower) |
| Rent growth (avg/yr) | 3.5% | 3.2% | −0.3 pts/yr (slightly behind) |
| Going-in cap rate | 5.25% | 5.25% (at acquisition) | in line |
| Capex / renovation | $18.0k/unit | $19.1k/unit | +$1.1k/unit (modestly over) |

Sources: exit cap and exit price from the sale closing statement and broker valuation; lease-up
and rent actuals from property-management trailing reports reconciled in fund accounting; capex
from the final cost report.

## Where the Variance Concentrates
From the numbers alone: nearly the entire IRR beat traces to the exit — the asset sold at a 5.10%
cap against a 5.75% underwritten exit, lifting exit value well above plan. The operating
assumptions ran the *other* way: stabilization took 18 months vs. 12, and rent growth came in
slightly light, both of which were drags on the hold-period cash flows. Observation only — the
attribution stage explains why and splits skill from market.

## Gaps
- The 5.10% exit cap reflects the 2024 transaction market; we did not isolate, in this record, how
  much of that is sector-wide compression vs. asset-specific. Attribution should make that call.
- The lease-up delay overlaps a 2020 disruption to renovation/leasing schedules; the record does
  not separate pandemic-specific delay from a standing optimism in our lease-up assumption.
