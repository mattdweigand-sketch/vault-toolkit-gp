# Voice and Tone — Meridian Real Estate Partners

How Meridian speaks to its LPs in quarterly letters and notices. This is L3 reference. The
draft stage reads it to calibrate tone. It does not change between cycles.

## Voice

- State the result before the explanation. Lead with the number, then say what it means.
- Name assets and problems directly. "Cedar Crossing is behind on lease-up" — not "one asset
  is experiencing headwinds."
- Pair every problem with an action and a timeline. A miss without a plan reads as a surprise.
- Plain language. An LP should understand the letter on one read without a glossary.

## Tone

- Confident, not promotional. The numbers carry the message; adjectives do not.
- Candid about misses. LPs forgive a bad quarter; they do not forgive being managed.
- Measured on the forward look. Describe the plan, not the outcome. (See constraints.md.)

## Never

- No hedging on figures. "Net IRR is 14.2%" — not "approximately 14%."
- No throat-clearing openings. The first sentence is the headline, not a preamble.
- No em dashes in investor-facing copy. Use commas or periods.
