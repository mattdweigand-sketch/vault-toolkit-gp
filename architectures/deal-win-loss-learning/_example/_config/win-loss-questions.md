# Canonical Win/Loss Questions — Ridgeline Capital Partners

This is the filled version for the worked example. The fixed set every analysis answers, in the
same order, every time. Comparability across records is what lets the store reveal patterns.
Question 5 (stated vs. assessed reason) is load-bearing — it is the defense against capturing
broker spin as the real reason.

## The Questions
1. How did this opportunity reach us, and through which broker/intermediary or off-market path?
2. What was our thesis, and the price/structure we bid?
3. What was the winning bid / clearing price, and our gap to it — in price *and* in terms?
4. On what dimension was it won or lost: price, certainty/speed of close, structure, financing,
   reputation/relationship, or timing?
5. Broker's *stated* reason vs. our *assessed* real reason? (Keep the two distinct and say which
   you believe.)
6. Did we bid with full information, or blind on a key term?
7. Where in the process did we gain or lose ground (first round, best-and-final, last look)?
8. Did our diligence / closing reputation help or hurt?
9. If lost: what realistically would have changed it — and at what price would it have stopped
   being a deal we wanted?
10. If won: what was the decisive edge, and what nearly lost it?
11. What would we do differently in a similar process (a relationship to build, a term to
    pre-clear, a faster path to certainty)?

## Answering Rules
- Answer every question, in order. Use "unknown" where the record does not support an answer —
  never fabricate to fill the slot.
- Keep answers tight and factual; the why and the lessons have their own sections in the analysis.
- Distinguish the broker's stated reason from your assessed reason explicitly, and never collapse
  them into one.
